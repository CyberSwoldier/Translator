# Norwegian Bokmål (nb) — Translation & Grammar Reference

## Overview
Norwegian Bokmål is the dominant written standard in Norway (used by ~90% of the population). It is regulated by the Norwegian Language Council (Språkrådet). Always use Bokmål, NOT Nynorsk, for broad Norwegian audiences.

---

## 1. REGISTER & TONE (Critical for phishing simulations)

- Norwegian professional communication is **notably informal** compared to other European languages. Formal "De" (capitalized) is archaic and rarely used — avoid it unless mimicking very old-fashioned institutional writing.
- Use **"du"** (lowercase) as the standard second-person form in almost all contexts, including emails and official-looking communications.
- Phishing emails should feel **natural and slightly urgent** — Norwegians respond well to direct, concise language without excessive formality.
- Greetings: "Hei," followed by the recipient's name is standard. NOT "Kjære" (too formal/archaic) unless intentionally mimicking older correspondence.
- No comma after "Hei" alone — only between "Hei" and the name: **Hei, Kari.**

---

## 2. NOUNS & GENDER

- Norwegian Bokmål has **three grammatical genders**: masculine (en), feminine (ei), neuter (et).
- In practice, **masculine and feminine are often merged** — using "en" for both is widely accepted and safe.
- Definite suffix is added to the END of the noun (postfix article):
  - en bil → bilen (the car)
  - et hus → huset (the house)
- Nouns in Bokmål have 8 forms (indefinite/definite × singular/plural × nominative/genitive).
- **Compound nouns** are written as ONE word: "passordtilbakestilling" (password reset), NOT "passord tilbakestilling".
- Norwegian tends to form longer compound words than English — always join them.

---

## 3. PRONOUNS

| Person | Subject | Object |
|--------|---------|--------|
| I | jeg | meg |
| you (sg) | du | deg |
| he | han | ham/han |
| she | hun | henne |
| we | vi | oss |
| you (pl) | dere | dere |
| they | de | dem |

- Formal "De" (capitalized) is essentially obsolete. Use "du" universally.
- Possessives typically follow the noun: "bilen min" (my car), but "min bil" is also acceptable.

---

## 4. VERBS

- Verbs do **NOT** conjugate by person or number — one form for all persons in each tense.
- Present tense: typically infinitive + -r → "å gå" → "går"
- Past tense: regular verbs add -te or -de → "sendte" (sent), "lagde" (made)
- Perfect tense: har + past participle → "har sendt" (has sent)
- Future: "skal" + infinitive (will/shall) or "vil" + infinitive (want/will)
- **V2 rule**: The finite verb must always be the **second element** in a main clause, even when an adverb or object is fronted.
  - "I går sendte vi en e-post." (Yesterday we sent an email.) NOT "I går vi sendte..."

---

## 5. ADJECTIVES

- Adjectives agree with the noun in gender and number.
- Indefinite singular: en stor bil (a big car) / et stort hus (a big house)
- Definite and plural: add -e → den store bilen / store biler
- Adjectives precede the noun.

---

## 6. SENTENCE STRUCTURE

- Basic order: **Subject-Verb-Object (SVO)** — same as English.
- **V2 rule** applies: verb is always in second position.
- Negation: "ikke" comes after the finite verb → "Jeg sendte ikke e-posten."
- Questions: verb comes first → "Sendte du e-posten?"

---

## 7. PUNCTUATION & FORMATTING

- **Decimal separator**: comma (2,5) NOT period.
- **Thousands separator**: space or period (1 000 or 1.000).
- No full stop after headings or titles.
- Quotation marks: «guillemets» or "standard double quotes" — both acceptable.
- Capitalisation: Only proper nouns and sentence starts. Days of the week, months, languages, and nationalities are **lowercase** in Norwegian: "mandag", "norsk", "norsk".
- Email greetings: no comma after closing before name → "Med vennlig hilsen\nKari"

---

## 8. NUMBERS & CURRENCY

- "Billion" in English = "milliard" in Norwegian (NOT "billion" — false friend!).
- Currency: kr or NOK. Write "100 kr" or "kr 100".
- Percentages: "50 %" (space before %).

---

## 9. KEY TRANSLATION PITFALLS

- "Please" → "Vennligst" or restructure: "Klikk her for å..." (avoid over-literal translation)
- "Click here" → "Klikk her"
- "Your account" → "kontoen din"
- "Verify" → "bekreft" or "verifiser"
- "Urgent" → "haster" or "Viktig"
- "Immediately" → "umiddelbart" or "straks"
- English passive voice → often better as active in Norwegian
- "has been" → restructure to active where possible

---

## 10. SPECIAL CHARACTERS

Must use correct Norwegian characters: **æ, ø, å** (lowercase) and **Æ, Ø, Å** (uppercase). These are NOT optional — omitting them produces misspellings.
