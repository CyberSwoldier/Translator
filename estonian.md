# Estonian (et) — Translation & Grammar Reference

## Overview
Estonian is a Finno-Ugric language spoken by ~1.1 million people in Estonia. Like Finnish, it is agglutinative with an extensive case system and NO grammatical gender. It has 14 noun cases, NO definite or indefinite articles, and NO future tense verb form. Estonian has been significantly influenced by German, Swedish, and Russian due to its history.

---

## 1. REGISTER & TONE (Critical for phishing simulations)

- Estonian professional tone is **formal and measured** compared to Nordic languages — a reflection of the more formal continental European influence.
- **Formal "Teie/Te"** (capitalized): used in official letters, formal emails, customer service, government communications. This is the correct formal address.
- **Informal "sina/sa"**: used among colleagues, friends, informal contexts.
- For phishing simulations targeting employees/customers: use **Teie** for institutional emails.
- Greeting (formal): "Lugupeetud [Title/Name]," or "Austatud [Name],"
- Greeting (standard): "Tere [Name]," or "Hea [Name],"
- Closing (formal): "Lugupidamisega" (with respect) or "Parimate soovidega" (with best wishes).
- "Mr/Mrs" titles (Härra/Proua) are NOT commonly used in Estonian — use name only.

---

## 2. NOUNS — THE CASE SYSTEM

Estonian has **14 grammatical cases** (no definite/indefinite articles):

| Case | Example | Usage |
|------|---------|-------|
| Nominative | maja (house) | Subject |
| Genitive | maja | Possession, direct object (complete) |
| Partitive | maja | Partial/indefinite object, after numbers |
| Illative | majja | Into |
| Inessive | majas | In |
| Elative | majast | Out of, from |
| Allative | majale | Onto, to |
| Adessive | majal | On, at |
| Ablative | majalt | Off, from |
| Translative | majaks | Becoming |
| Terminative | majani | As far as, until |
| Essive | majana | As, in the capacity of |
| Abessive | majata | Without |
| Comitative | majaga | With, together with |

- NO grammatical gender — "tema" (ta) means both "he" and "she".
- **Consonant gradation** (like Finnish) applies: consonants alternate between strong and weak grades.
- **Three vowel lengths**: short, long, overlong — distinctive and meaningful. Long vowels written double: "maa" (land), "keel" (language).

---

## 3. PRONOUNS

- Estonian is **pro-drop** — subject pronouns may be omitted.
- Gender-neutral: "tema" / "ta" = he/she/it. No distinction between masculine and feminine.
- Formal address: **Teie** (capitalized, singular or plural formal).
- Personal pronouns:
  - mina/ma (I), sina/sa (you), tema/ta (he/she), meie/me (we), teie/te (you pl/formal), nemad/nad (they).
- Possessives: formed with genitive of pronoun + noun: "minu konto" (my account).

---

## 4. VERBS

- Verbs conjugate by person and number.
- **NO future tense** — present tense + context indicates future: "ma saadan teile vastuse" (I will send you a reply) literally "I send you a reply."
- Key tenses: present, past (imperfect), perfect (olen + past participle), pluperfect (olin + past participle).
- Present conjugation of "olema" (to be):
  - mina olen | sina oled | tema on | meie oleme | teie olete | nemad on
- Negative form: "ei" + verb (verb does NOT take personal endings in negative):
  - Ma ei räägi (I don't speak) | Sa ei räägi | Ta ei räägi...
- Passive: common in formal/written Estonian. Formed with -takse/-dakse.
- **Formal imperative**: use the plural form (-ge/-ke): "Klõpsake siin" (Click here — formal).

---

## 5. SENTENCE STRUCTURE

- Basic order **SVO**, but flexible due to case system.
- Non-neutral word order emphasizes specific elements.
- Adjectives agree in case and number with the noun they modify.
- Adjectives precede nouns.

---

## 6. PUNCTUATION & FORMATTING

- **Decimal separator**: comma (2,5). **Thousands separator**: space (1 000).
- Quotation marks: „bottom-top" style „Estonian quotes" (preferred) or "standard double".
- Mathematical operators have space around them.
- Zero before decimal: English may omit it ("0.5"), Estonian must include it ("0,5").
- Negative numbers: no space after minus sign.
- Capitalisation: Only proper nouns and sentence starts. Days, months, languages LOWERCASE: "esmaspäev", "eesti".
- Acronyms: add case ending based on pronunciation. If read as a word, directly; if spelled out, with colon: "NATO" or "NATO-t" (partitive), "EU" → "EL" (Estonian abbreviation for Euroopa Liit).

---

## 7. NUMBERS & CURRENCY

- "Billion" (10^9) = "miljard" in Estonian. NOT "biljon" (false friend).
- Currency: € or EUR. "100 eurot" or "100 €".

---

## 8. KEY TRANSLATION PITFALLS

- "Please" → restructure: "Palun klõpsake..." (Please click...) — "palun" can be used as a softener but rethink the sentence structure.
- "Click here" → "Klõpsake siin" (formal) or "Klõpsa siin" (informal).
- "Your account" → "teie konto" (formal) or "sinu konto" (informal). No article.
- "Verify" → "kinnitage" (formal) or "kontrollige".
- "Urgent" → "Kiire" or "Tähtis".
- "Log in" → "Logige sisse" (formal) or "Logi sisse".
- "Password" → "parool" or "salasõna".
- "Username" → "kasutajanimi".
- English passive → Estonian formal writing uses passive frequently, so it can be maintained.
- "Mr/Mrs" → Do NOT translate; use name directly.

---

## 9. SPECIAL CHARACTERS

Must use: **ä, ö, ü, õ** (and uppercase **Ä, Ö, Ü, Õ**). "Õ" is unique to Estonian and critical — it is a mid-back unrounded vowel. Also: š, ž (used in loanwords). All special characters are mandatory.
