# Finnish (fi) — Translation & Grammar Reference

## Overview
Finnish is a Finno-Ugric (Uralic) language — NOT Indo-European — spoken by ~5 million people in Finland. It is one of the most structurally different languages from English. Finnish is agglutinative (meaning grammatical information is added as suffixes to word stems) and has NO grammatical gender, NO articles, and NO future tense as a verb form.

---

## 1. REGISTER & TONE (Critical for phishing simulations)

- Finnish professional communication is **direct and concise** — Finns typically dislike verbose or overly polite language.
- **Formal "Te"** (capitalized) is used in formal/official letters, legal documents, and respectful correspondence. Use it for phishing simulations mimicking official institutions (banks, government, large corporations).
- **Informal "sinä/sie"** (or omitted, as Finnish is pro-drop) is standard in most commercial emails.
- For phishing simulations targeting employees/customers: use **Te** (formal) for institutional-sounding emails, **sinä** (or no pronoun) for casual/service emails.
- Greeting: "Hyvä [Title/Name]," (formal) or "Hei [Name]," (standard/friendly).
- Closing: "Ystävällisin terveisin" (formal: with friendly regards) or "Terveisin" (standard: regards).
- After a greeting followed by a comma: if the next sentence starts with a lowercase letter, use the comma. If uppercase, remove comma or replace with exclamation mark.

---

## 2. NOUNS — THE CASE SYSTEM

Finnish has **15 grammatical cases**. These replace prepositions and articles. There is NO definite or indefinite article ("the", "a"). Key cases:

| Case | Suffix example | Usage |
|------|---------------|-------|
| Nominative | talo (house) | Subject |
| Genitive | talon | Possession, object of certain verbs |
| Accusative | talon / talon | Complete object |
| Partitive | taloa | Partial object, indefinite quantities, after numbers 2+ |
| Inessive | talossa | In, inside (in the house) |
| Elative | talosta | Out of, from inside (from the house) |
| Illative | taloon | Into (into the house) |
| Adessive | talolla | At, on (at/on the house) |
| Ablative | talolta | From (from the house) |
| Allative | talolle | To, onto (to the house) |
| Essive | talona | As, in the role of |
| Translative | taloksi | Becoming, turning into |

- **Vowel harmony**: Finnish vowels divide into "front" (ä, ö, y) and "back" (a, o, u). Within a word, suffixes must match the stem's vowel category. "talo" (back) → talossa; "pöytä" (front) → pöydässä.
- **Consonant gradation**: consonants alternate between "strong" and "weak" grades depending on the case. E.g., "kukka" (flower) → "kukan" (genitive).
- No grammatical gender whatsoever — "hän" means both "he" and "she".

---

## 3. PRONOUNS

- Finnish is **pro-drop** — subject pronouns are often omitted since verb endings encode person.
- In formal written Finnish, "minä" (I), "sinä" (you), "hän" (he/she), "me" (we), "te" (you pl/formal), "he" (they) are used.
- In colloquial Finnish: "mä" (I), "sä" (you), "se" (he/she), "ne" (they) — avoid in formal translations.
- "Hän" = both he AND she. Never use "hän" for objects or animals in formal writing.

---

## 4. VERBS

- Verbs conjugate by person and number.
- **NO future tense** — use present tense + context: "palaamme asiaan" (we will get back to you) literally "we return to the matter."
- The **tulla tekemään** structure ("to come to do") may occasionally express future but is rare.
- Tenses: present/nonpast, past (imperfect), perfect (olen + past participle), pluperfect (olin + past participle).
- Passive voice: exists but is less common than in Germanic languages. Active preferred where possible.
- Key verb endings (present):
  - minä: puhu**n** | sinä: puhu**t** | hän: puhu**u** | me: puhu**mme** | te: puhu**tte** | he: puhu**vat**

---

## 5. NEGATION

- Negation uses a **negative verb** "ei" (conjugated) before the main verb:
  - En puhu (I don't speak) | Et puhu | Ei puhu | Emme puhu | Ette puhu | Eivät puhu
- Double negation is grammatically valid in Finnish for emphasis but does NOT create a positive meaning.

---

## 6. SENTENCE STRUCTURE

- Basic order **SVO**, but very flexible due to case system.
- Word order changes indicate emphasis, not grammatical role.
- Adjectives precede nouns and agree in case and number with the noun.

---

## 7. PUNCTUATION & FORMATTING

- **Decimal separator**: comma (2,5). **Thousands separator**: non-breaking space (1 000).
- Quotation marks: "double standard" quotes preferred (not guillemets).
- After greeting in emails: comma only if the next line starts lowercase. If uppercase, no comma or use exclamation mark.
- No comma after closing before sender's name.
- Capitalisation: Only sentence starts and proper nouns. Months, days, languages LOWERCASE: "maanantai", "suomi".
- Acronyms: if read as a word, add case ending directly (EU:ssa → EUssa possible); if spelled out, add colon + case ending (NATO:n, EU:n).

---

## 8. NUMBERS & CURRENCY

- "Billion" (10^9) = "miljardi" in Finnish. NOT "biljoona" (= 10^12 in Finnish).
- Currency: € or EUR. Written as "100 €" or "100 euroa".
- Thousands separator is a non-breaking space: 1 000 000.

---

## 9. KEY TRANSLATION PITFALLS

- "Please" → restructure entirely: "Kirjaudu sisään" (Log in), "Vahvista tilisi" (Verify your account) — no direct Finnish equivalent for "please" as a sentence opener.
- "Click here" → "Napsauta tästä" or "Klikkaa tästä"
- "Your account" → "tilisi" (your account, no article)
- "Verify" → "vahvista"
- "Urgent" → "Kiireellinen" or "Tärkeää"
- "Log in" → "Kirjaudu sisään"
- "Password" → "salasana"
- "Username" → "käyttäjänimi"
- English passive constructions → prefer Finnish active.
- "The [noun]" → just the noun with appropriate case ending (no article).

---

## 10. SPECIAL CHARACTERS

Must use: **ä, ö** (and uppercase **Ä, Ö**). Also "å" exists in some loanwords/names. These are mandatory — omitting creates incorrect Finnish.
