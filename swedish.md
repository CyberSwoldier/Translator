# Swedish (sv) — Translation & Grammar Reference

## Overview
Swedish is spoken by ~10 million people, primarily in Sweden. The standard written form is "rikssvenska". It is a North Germanic language regulated informally by the Swedish Academy (Svenska Akademien). Swedish is notably accessible for English speakers due to structural similarities.

---

## 1. REGISTER & TONE (Critical for phishing simulations)

- Swedish has undergone a significant **"du-reform"** since the 1970s — "ni" (formal "you") is NO LONGER standard and should almost never be used. Using "ni" to address a single person sounds outdated and may seem mocking.
- **ALWAYS use "du"** for second-person singular in any context — professional, commercial, email, official.
- English "you" or "your" should NEVER be translated as "ni" or "er". Use **du / dig / din / ditt / dina**.
- Tone in professional Swedish is **direct, clear, and moderately warm** — not excessively formal.
- Phishing emails should feel natural and conversational but with clear urgency.
- Greetings: "Hej," + name is standard. "Bäste [Name]" is formal (letter style only).
- Closing: "Med vänliga hälsningar" or "Vänligen" for formal, "Hälsningar" for standard.

---

## 2. NOUNS & GENDER

- Swedish has **TWO grammatical genders**: common gender (utrum, "en-words") and neuter (neutrum, "ett-words").
- ~75–80% of nouns are common gender (en-words). When uncertain, "en" is the safer choice.
- Definite article is a **SUFFIX** added to the noun:
  - en bil → bilen (the car)
  - ett hus → huset (the house)
  - plural common: bilar → bilarna (the cars)
  - plural neuter: hus → husen (the houses)
- Compound nouns are ONE word: "lösenordsåterställning" (password reset).
- Genitive: add -s to the end of the word (no apostrophe!) → "bilens" (the car's).

## 3. PRONOUNS

| Person | Subject | Object | Possessive (en/ett/pl) |
|--------|---------|--------|----------------------|
| I | jag | mig | min / mitt / mina |
| you (sg) | du | dig | din / ditt / dina |
| he | han | honom | hans |
| she | hon | henne | hennes |
| it (common) | den | den | dess |
| it (neuter) | det | det | dess |
| we | vi | oss | vår / vårt / våra |
| you (formal/pl) | ni | er | er / ert / era |
| they | de (dom) | dem (dom) | deras |

- **"de" and "dem"**: both pronounced "dom" in spoken Swedish. In writing: "de" = subject (they), "dem" = object (them). Machine-translated text often confuses these — verify.
- Gender-neutral singular pronoun **"hen"** (like singular "they") exists for gender-neutral contexts.

---

## 4. VERBS

- Verbs do **NOT** conjugate by person or number in modern Swedish.
- Present tense: infinitive typically ends in -a; present adds -r → "att skicka" → "skickar"
- Past tense: varies by verb group (most common: add -de or -te)
- Perfect: "har" + supine → "har skickat" (has sent)
- Future: "ska" + infinitive (will) or "kommer att" + infinitive (going to)
- **V2 rule**: Finite verb must be the SECOND element in the main clause.
  - "Igår skickade vi ett mejl." (Yesterday we sent an email.)
- Swedish uses **passive voice** more than English: formed by adding -s to verb → "skickas" (is sent).

---

## 5. ADJECTIVES

- Agree with noun in gender and definiteness.
- Indefinite common: en stor bil | Indefinite neuter: ett stort hus | Plural/definite: stora
- Definite adjective adds -e for singular masculine/common when natural gender is known (rare).
- Adjectives precede the noun.
- Comparative: add -are → "större" (bigger); Superlative: add -ast → "störst" (biggest).

---

## 6. SENTENCE STRUCTURE

- **SVO** basic order, but strict **V2 rule** applies in main clauses.
- Negation: "inte" comes AFTER the finite verb → "Jag skickade inte mejlet."
- In subordinate clauses, negation comes BEFORE the verb → "...att jag inte skickade mejlet."
- Questions: verb-first → "Skickade du mejlet?"

---

## 7. PUNCTUATION & FORMATTING

- **Decimal separator**: comma (2,5).
- **Thousands separator**: space (10 000).
- Quotation marks: "typographic" or »Swedish style« — standard double quotes also acceptable.
- Capitalisation: Only sentence starts and proper nouns. Days, months, languages LOWERCASE: "måndag", "svenska".
- No full stop after headings.
- Email: no comma after closing line before name.

---

## 8. NUMBERS & CURRENCY

- Hundreds: "hundra" NOT "ett hundra" (though "ett hundra" is accepted).
- "Billion" = "miljard" (NOT "billion" — false friend!). "Billion" in Swedish = 10^12 (one trillion in English).
- Currency: kr or SEK. "100 kr" or "kr 100".
- Percentages: "50 %" (space before %).

---

## 9. KEY TRANSLATION PITFALLS

- "Please" → restructure naturally: "Klicka här för att..." or "Vänligen klicka..."
- "Click here" → "Klicka här"
- "Your account" → "ditt konto"
- "Verify" → "verifiera" or "bekräfta"
- "Urgent" → "Brådskande" or "Viktigt"
- "Log in" → "logga in"
- "Password" → "lösenord"
- "Username" → "användarnamn"
- Avoid over-literal passive constructions — prefer active Swedish.

---

## 10. SPECIAL CHARACTERS

Must use: **å, ä, ö** (and uppercase **Å, Ä, Ö**). These are mandatory — omitting them produces incorrect Swedish.
