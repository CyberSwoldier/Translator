# Latvian (lv) — Translation & Grammar Reference

## Overview
Latvian is a Baltic (Indo-European) language spoken by ~1.75 million people in Latvia. It is one of only two surviving Baltic languages (alongside Lithuanian). Latvian is an inflected language with 7 noun cases, 2 grammatical genders (masculine/feminine), and no articles. It retains many archaic Indo-European features.

---

## 1. REGISTER & TONE (Critical for phishing simulations)

- Latvian formal tone is **moderately formal** — more formal than Nordic languages but less ceremonious than Polish.
- **Formal "Jūs/Jums"** (capitalized): used in official emails, letters, customer communications. When addressing one person formally, "Jūs" means singular "you" (not plural) and is capitalized.
- **Informal "tu/tev"**: used among colleagues, friends, casual contexts.
- For phishing simulations: use **Jūs** for institutional/official-looking emails, "tu" for casual service emails.
- Greeting (formal): "Cienījamais [Name]!" or "Labdien, [Name]!" — note the **exclamation mark** after greetings in Latvian letters/emails.
- Closing: "Ar cieņu," (With respect) or "Ar labākajiem novēlējumiem," (With best wishes).
- **Important**: In Latvian, greetings in letters/emails MUST be followed by an exclamation mark, NOT a comma alone.

---

## 2. NOUNS & GENDER

- Latvian has **TWO grammatical genders**: masculine and feminine (no neuter).
- Masculine nouns typically end in -s or -is.
- Feminine nouns typically end in -a or -e.
- **SEVEN cases**: nominative, genitive, dative, accusative, instrumental, locative, vocative.
  - Instrumental is always identical to accusative (singular) or dative (plural) — rarely used as standalone in modern Latvian.
  - Vocative is used for direct address.
- **NO articles** — definiteness is indicated by adjective endings:
  - Indefinite adjective: "jauna māja" (a new house) — short ending
  - Definite adjective: "jaunā māja" (the new house) — long ending with macron
- Adjectives agree with nouns in gender, number, and case, and also in definiteness.
- Plural formation: masculine -s → -i; feminine -a → -as.

---

## 3. PRONOUNS

| Person | Nominative | Genitive/Possessive |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| I | es | mana/mans/mani |
| you (informal sg) | tu | tava/tavs |
| he | viņš | viņa |
| she | viņa | viņas |
| we | mēs | mūsu |
| you (formal/pl) | Jūs (formal) / jūs | Jūsu (formal) / jūsu |
| they (m) | viņi | viņu |
| they (f) | viņas | viņu |

- Formal "Jūs" and possessive "Jūsu" are always **capitalized** when addressing one person formally.
- Unlike Norwegian/Swedish/Danish, Latvian does NOT generally omit subject pronouns.
- However, redundant pronouns can be omitted when context is clear: "Sazināšos ar Jums" (I will contact you) — "es" omitted.

---

## 4. VERBS

- Verbs conjugate by person and number.
- Three tenses: present, past, future (unlike Finnish/Estonian, Latvian HAS a future tense).
- Present: "es rakstu" (I write), "tu raksti", "viņš/viņa raksta", "mēs rakstām", "jūs rakstāt", "viņi/viņas raksta".
- Past: add -āju/-ēju/-iju etc. depending on conjugation class.
- Future: add -šu/-si/-s/-sim/-siet/-s (complex alternations).
- Conditional: ending in -tu.
- Passive voice: analytic, using auxiliary "tikt"/"būt" + past passive participle.
- **Future perfect** tense exists and may be incorrectly translated — pay attention.
- Cases must be correct per verb requirements — a common machine translation error is keeping nouns in nominative when they should be in another case.

---

## 5. ADJECTIVES

- Agree with noun in gender, number, case, AND definiteness.
- Definite adjectives use macrons (long vowels) on endings: "lielā māja" (the big house).
- Indefinite: "liela māja" (a big house).
- Comparative: add -āks (m) / -āka (f) → "lielāks" (bigger).
- Superlative: add vis- prefix + comparative → "vislielākais" (the biggest).

---

## 6. SENTENCE STRUCTURE

- Basic **SVO** order, but flexible due to the case system.
- Word order changes emphasize different elements.
- Negation: prefix "ne-" added to verb → "nerakstu" (I don't write) or "nav" (is not).
- Double negation is required: "Es nekad nerakstu" (I never write) — literally "I never not-write."
- Questions: yes/no questions use "vai" at the start → "Vai Jūs rakstāt?" (Do you write?).

---

## 7. PUNCTUATION & FORMATTING

- **Decimal separator**: comma (2,5). **Thousands separator**: space (1 000).
- **Exclamation mark** mandatory after salutations in emails/letters: "Labdien, Kārli!"
- No articles — definiteness shown through adjective endings.
- Quotation marks: "standard" double quotes or „bottom-top" style.
- Capitalisation: Only proper nouns and sentence starts. Days, months, languages LOWERCASE: "pirmdiena", "latviešu".

---

## 8. NUMBERS & CURRENCY

- "Billion" (10^9) = "miljards" in Latvian.
- Currency: € or EUR. "100 eiro" or "100 €".
- "Dollar" → "dolārs".

---

## 9. KEY TRANSLATION PITFALLS

- "Please" → "Lūdzu" (can be used as sentence starter or softener).
- "Click here" → "Noklikšķiniet šeit" (formal) or "Klikšķini šeit" (informal).
- "Your account" → "Jūsu konts" (formal) or "tavs konts" (informal).
- "Verify" → "apstipriniet" (formal) or "verificējiet".
- "Urgent" → "Steidzami" or "Svarīgi".
- "Log in" → "Piesakieties" (formal) or "Pieslēdzieties".
- "Password" → "parole".
- "Username" → "lietotājvārds".
- Prepositions govern specific cases — machine translation often gets this wrong.
- Keep nouns in the correct case based on the governing verb or preposition.

---

## 10. SPECIAL CHARACTERS

Must use: **ā, č, ē, ģ, ī, ķ, ļ, ņ, š, ū, ž** (and their uppercase equivalents). Macrons (ā, ē, ī, ū) indicate long vowels and are grammatically meaningful — they are NOT optional. Omitting them changes word meaning and produces incorrect Latvian.
