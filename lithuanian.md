# Lithuanian (lt) — Translation & Grammar Reference

## Overview
Lithuanian is a Baltic (Indo-European) language spoken by ~3 million people in Lithuania. It is widely regarded as the most archaic surviving Indo-European language, retaining many features lost in most other languages. Lithuanian is richly inflected with 7 noun cases, 2 grammatical genders, and a complex verb system. It has been influenced by German, Polish, and Russian, though a Language Commission actively maintains its purity.

---

## 1. REGISTER & TONE (Critical for phishing simulations)

- Lithuanian formal tone is **clearly distinct from informal** — choosing the right register is essential.
- **Formal "Jūs/Jums/Jūsų"** (capitalized): mandatory for official communications, institutional emails, customer service. When addressing one person formally, "Jūs" is singular but capitalized.
- **Informal "tu/tau/tavo"**: used among colleagues, friends.
- For phishing simulations: use **Jūs** (formal, capitalized) for institutional-looking emails.
- Greeting (formal): "Gerbiamasis [male name]," / "Gerbiamoji [female name]," or "Laba diena, [Name],"
- Closing (formal): "Pagarbiai," (Respectfully) or "Su pagarba," (With respect).
- **Gender agreement in greetings**: "Gerbiamasis" (masculine) vs "Gerbiamoji" (feminine) — must match recipient gender.

---

## 2. NOUNS & GENDER

- Lithuanian has **TWO grammatical genders**: masculine (vyriškoji giminė) and feminine (moteriškoji giminė). No neuter gender for nouns (though neuter forms exist for some adjectives/pronouns).
- Gender affects all agreement: verbs (past tense), adjectives, past participles.
- **SEVEN cases** (plus a rare vocative for address):
  - Nominative, Genitive, Dative, Accusative, Instrumental, Locative, Vocative
- Cases are expressed through noun endings, which vary by declension class and gender.
- Masculine nouns typically end in -as, -is, -us, -ias, or -ys.
- Feminine nouns typically end in -a, -ė, -ia, -is, -uo.
- All cases except vocative can carry multiple meanings depending on context.
- **NO articles** in Lithuanian.

---

## 3. PRONOUNS

| Person | Nominative | Dative | Possessive |
|--------|-----------|--------|------------|
| I | aš | man | mano |
| you (informal) | tu | tau | tavo |
| he | jis | jam | jo |
| she | ji | jai | jos |
| we | mes | mums | mūsų |
| you (formal/pl) | Jūs | Jums | Jūsų |
| they (m) | jie | jiems | jų |
| they (f) | jos | joms | jų |

- Formal "Jūs", "Jums", "Jūsų" are always **capitalized** when addressing one person formally.
- Lithuanian is **pro-drop** — subject pronouns are often omitted since verbs encode person and gender.
- **3rd person has NO distinction by number** in verb forms — "jis eina" and "jie eina" use the same 3rd person verb form.
- Gender agreement is critical: past tense verbs and adjectives must agree with subject gender.

---

## 4. VERBS

- Verbs conjugate by person, number, tense, and mood.
- **Three tenses**: present, past (simple), past frequentative (habitual past).
- Future: formed with -s- infix or special future forms.
- Perfect tenses: compound (yra/buvo + participle).
- Past tense agrees with SUBJECT GENDER:
  - Masculine: "jis rašė" (he wrote) | Feminine: "ji rašė" (she wrote) — endings differ in many forms.
- Gender-neutral writing: when gender is unknown, Lithuanian requires expressing both genders (e.g., "pasirašęs(-iusi)") or using "Prašome" + verb structure.
- Imperative:
  - Singular informal: verb root (e.g., "rašyk!" — write!)
  - Formal/plural: add -kite (e.g., "rašykite!" — write! [formal])
  - "Prašome" + infinitive is the most neutral formal request form.

---

## 5. ADJECTIVES

- Agree with nouns in gender, number, and case.
- Masculine adjectives: typically end in -as, -us, -is.
- Feminine adjectives: typically end in -a, -i, -ė.
- Comparative: add -esnis (m) / -esnė (f) → "didesnis" (bigger).
- Superlative: add didžiausias (biggest) — formed with -iausias (m) / -iausia (f).
- Adjectives precede nouns.

---

## 6. SENTENCE STRUCTURE

- Basic **SVO** order, but flexible due to inflection — changes in word order signal emphasis.
- Negation: "ne-" prefix on verb → "nerašau" (I don't write), or "nėra" (is not/there is not).
- Questions: yes/no questions use rising intonation in speech; in writing, question mark. No "vai"-like particle — questions formed by inversion or question words.
- Coordinated clauses must have adjectives/verbs aligned in gender and number with the noun they refer to — machine translation often fails here.

---

## 7. PUNCTUATION & FORMATTING

- **Decimal separator**: comma (2,5). **Thousands separator**: space (1 000).
- Quotation marks: „bottom-top" style „Lithuanian quotes" (preferred).
- Capitalisation: Only proper nouns and sentence starts. Days, months, languages LOWERCASE: "pirmadienis", "lietuvių".
- Formal salutations may use comma after: "Gerbiamasis Tomas,"

---

## 8. NUMBERS & CURRENCY

- "Billion" (10^9) = "milijardas" in Lithuanian.
- Currency: € or EUR. "100 eurų" (genitive plural after numbers).
- Cardinal numbers require genitive after 2, 3, 4 and genitive plural after 5+.

---

## 9. KEY TRANSLATION PITFALLS

- "Please" → "Prašome" (formal softener before imperative) or restructure entirely.
- "Click here" → "Spustelėkite čia" (formal) or "Spauskite čia".
- "Your account" → "Jūsų paskyra" (formal) or "tavo paskyra" (informal).
- "Verify" → "patvirtinkite" (formal) or "patikrinkite".
- "Urgent" → "Skubu" or "Svarbu".
- "Log in" → "Prisijunkite" (formal) or "Prisijunk".
- "Password" → "slaptažodis".
- "Username" → "vartotojo vardas".
- **Gender agreement errors** are the #1 machine translation failure in Lithuanian — always verify that past tense verbs and adjectives match the subject's gender.
- Imperative in formal context: MUST use -kite form, never singular imperative.
- Cases must follow verb/preposition requirements — "nominative" default is wrong for most objects.

---

## 10. SPECIAL CHARACTERS

Must use: **ą, č, ę, ė, į, š, ų, ū, ž** (and uppercase **Ą, Č, Ę, Ė, Į, Š, Ų, Ū, Ž**). Ogonek (ą, ę, į, ų) and macron (ū) indicate phonological distinctions that are grammatically meaningful. These are NOT optional — omitting them produces incorrect Lithuanian.
