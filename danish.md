# Danish (da) — Translation & Grammar Reference

## Overview
Danish is spoken by ~6 million people in Denmark. It is a North Germanic language with close ties to Norwegian and Swedish. Danish writing is generally more conservative than spoken Danish. Danish is notable for its unique phonology including the "stød" (glottal stop) and many silent consonants.

---

## 1. REGISTER & TONE (Critical for phishing simulations)

- Like Norwegian and Swedish, Danish has abandoned formal "De" (capitalized) as standard address. Since the 1970s, **"du"** is used universally.
- "De" (formal) survives only in very specific formal letters or when addressing royalty — avoid it.
- Danish professional tone is **direct, friendly, and informal**. Excessive formality sounds unnatural.
- Phishing emails should feel natural and slightly urgent, using plain and accessible language.
- Greetings: "Hej," + name is universally appropriate. "Kære" + name is more formal (letter style).
- Closing: "Med venlig hilsen" (formal/standard) or "Mange hilsener" (warm).

---

## 2. NOUNS & GENDER

- Danish has **TWO grammatical genders**: common gender (fælleskøn, ~75% of nouns) and neuter (intetkøn).
- Common gender article: **en** | Neuter article: **et**
- Definite article is a **SUFFIX**:
  - en bil → bilen (the car) | et hus → huset (the house)
  - Plural common: biler → bilerne | Plural neuter: huse → husene
- When in doubt, use "en" (common gender covers ~75% of nouns).
- Compound nouns are ONE word: "adgangskodeopdatering" (password update).
- Genitive: add -s (no apostrophe) → "bilens" (the car's).

---

## 3. PRONOUNS

| Person | Subject | Object |
|--------|---------|--------|
| I | jeg | mig |
| you (sg) | du | dig |
| he | han | ham |
| she | hun | hende |
| we | vi | os |
| you (pl) | I | jer |
| they | de | dem |

- Reflexive pronoun: "sin/sit/sine" for same-subject possession vs. "hans/hendes/deres" for different subject — a common source of errors: "Han vasker sin bil" (his own car) vs. "Han vasker hans bil" (someone else's car).
- "Nogen" vs. "Nogle": "nogen" = anyone (questions, negatives, conditionals); "nogle" = some (plural, affirmative). This is a very common error.

---

## 4. VERBS

- Danish verbs do **NOT** conjugate by person or number.
- Present tense: add **-r** to the infinitive → "sende" → "sender" (sends/is sending). THE -R ENDING IS CRITICAL.
- Past tense: add -te or -ede → "sendte" (sent) or "modtog" (received, irregular).
- Perfect: "har" + past participle → "har sendt" (has sent).
- Future: "vil" + infinitive or "skal" + infinitive.
- **V2 rule**: Finite verb must be in SECOND position in main clauses.
  - "I går sendte vi en e-mail." NOT "I går vi sendte..."
- Key reminder: present tense -r ending: "Jeg sender" (I send/am sending) — omitting the -r is a VERY common mistake.

---

## 5. ADJECTIVES

- Agree in gender and definiteness.
- Indefinite common: en stor bil | Indefinite neuter: et stort hus | Plural/definite: store
- Comparative: add -ere → "større" | Superlative: add -est → "størst".
- No full stop after headings.

---

## 6. SENTENCE STRUCTURE

- Basic **SVO** order with strict **V2 rule**.
- Negation: "ikke" comes AFTER the finite verb in main clauses → "Jeg sendte ikke e-mailen."
- In subordinate clauses, "ikke" comes BEFORE the verb → "...at jeg ikke sendte..."
- Questions: verb first → "Sendte du e-mailen?"

---

## 7. PUNCTUATION & FORMATTING

- **Decimal separator**: comma (2,5). **Thousands separator**: period (1.000).
- English "billions" ≠ Danish "billioner" — "billions" (10^9) = **"milliarder"** in Danish. FALSE FRIEND — very important.
- Percentages: always write "%" with a preceding SPACE: "50 %".
- Comma rules: ALWAYS place comma between independent clauses (fixed final comma). Optional before dependent clause start.
- No full stops after headings.
- Quotation marks: »guillemets« (preferred) or "standard".
- Capitalisation: Only sentence start and proper nouns. Days, months, languages are LOWERCASE: "mandag", "dansk".

---

## 8. NUMBERS & CURRENCY

- Currency: kr or DKK. "100 kr" or "kr 100".
- Large numbers use period as thousands separator: 1.000.000.
- Written numbers: "hundrede" (hundred), "tusind" (thousand), "million", "milliard" (billion).

---

## 9. KEY TRANSLATION PITFALLS

- "Please" → restructure: "Klik her for at..." or "Venligst klik på..."
- "Click here" → "Klik her"
- "Your account" → "din konto"
- "Verify" → "bekræft" or "verificer"
- "Urgent" → "Haster" or "Vigtigt"
- "Log in" → "Log ind"
- "Password" → "adgangskode"
- "Username" → "brugernavn"
- "Immediately" → "straks" or "med det samme"
- Watch for English passive → prefer active in Danish.

---

## 10. SPECIAL CHARACTERS

Must use: **æ, ø, å** (and uppercase **Æ, Ø, Å**). Mandatory — omitting creates misspellings.
